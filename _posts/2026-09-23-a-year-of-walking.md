It was an absolute pleasure to close out a year of walking through internet infrastructure in London by returning where it all started at Open City's [Open House Festival](https://programme.openhouse.org.uk).  I thought I'd write a bit about how these walks started, as they've shape-shifted throughout the year.
  
I first gave this walk at the festival last year, after the encouragement of two friends, Brandon Jackson and Justin Manley – both of which are graduates of Open City's "[Golden Key Academy](https://open-city.org.uk/golden-key-academy)" . Little did I know that this would become a year-long experiment!

The original question behind the tour was: "How can I show people how to spot internet infrastructure around them?". I was inspired by the work (and walks!) of Ingrid Burrington, Mario Santamaría, and the critical infrastructure lab, who took their critical approaches to technology and brought them closer to the street, teaching walkers how to identify. It eventually became: "What is a story worth telling about the internet in the UK?"  
  
What started as a summer prototype (thanks to Brandon Jackson and Justin Manley - both graduates of Open City's "Golden Key" programme) became a year-long experiment. 

A year later, I've given walks for [****Open Knowledge Foundation****](https://www.linkedin.com/company/okfn/)'s Open Data Days, [****London Data Week****](https://www.linkedin.com/company/london-data-week/), [****Projects by IF****](https://www.linkedin.com/company/projects-by-if/), [****University of the Arts London****](https://www.linkedin.com/school/university-of-the-arts-london/) and [****London School of Solarpunk****](https://www.linkedin.com/company/londonschoolofsolarpunk/) and others, and a handful around a number of internet and tech-related "holidays".  
  
People have joined from all walks of life (so to speak): civil servants from councils, technologists from all sorts of companies, artists, researchers, engineers, infrastructure enthusiasts, journalists, and everyday folks interested in learning more about technology.  
  
Walkers have also become collaborators and friends, and some have even started giving walks themselves (spot: [****Michael Kibedi****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/uxmichaelco/) at [****Serpentine****](https://www.linkedin.com/company/serpentine-galleries/) a few weeks ago!). Each one was different, and brought another angle, question or research rabbit hole.  
  
Perhaps my biggest takeaway is how out of step the pace of technology and policy can be with how people actually understand something as ubiquitous as the internet. Amidst all the doomsday scenarios about AI and data centres, we still have a long way to go in explaining that the internet is more than “the cloud” – let alone figuring out how to regulate it.  
  
In any case, it's been a joy. Thank you for joining, for walking, and for listening.  
  
These walks will probably be a bit more ad-hoc from here on out, but I'm not quite done with them yet.
